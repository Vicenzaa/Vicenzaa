# Vicenza

**AI · Cognitive Systems · Product Design**

> **When technology meets human cognition.**

I’m interested in the space where **technology, cognition and complex systems** intersect.

I study how we can build systems that not only *perform*, but **represent, reason, explain and interact with humans**.

My work currently moves across **AI, computational biology and product design**, with a recurring interest in one thing:

**understanding the human within the system.**

### Research

🧠 **Neuro-Symbolic AI**
My Master's research explores a neuro-symbolic decision-support system for geopolitical contexts, combining **Knowledge Graphs, Graph Representation Learning and uncertainty** to create systems that can reason while making room for **explainability and human-in-the-loop decision making**.

🧬 **Computational Biology**
I'm exploring deep learning for **genomic and biological prediction**, including the use of **synthetic data to simulate multi-omic scenarios**.

### Beyond the model

I’m drawn to **XAI, world models, cognitive systems and human-computer interaction**.

When I design, I think about **cognition, perception, behavior and context** — not just interfaces.

When I build AI, I care about **what the model represents, how it reasons, what it doesn't know, and how humans can remain part of the process**.

Because somewhere beneath every technical system,
there is still a human being trying to **understand, decide, create or connect**.

And I think that's where things get interesting.

I like building things.

And I tend to get a little too curious about them.

Why does this work?
Why does someone interact with it this way?
What does the model actually *know*?
And what happens when it doesn't?

Somewhere along the way, I ended up exploring **AI, cognitive systems, design and computational science** — not really as separate fields, but as different ways of looking at the same thing.

I'm currently working on **neuro-symbolic AI**, with a particular interest in **XAI, world models, graph learning and uncertainty**, while also exploring **computational biology, genomics and synthetic multi-omics**.

When I design, I think about **how people perceive, understand and interact** with what we build.

When I work with AI, I keep coming back to the same question:

> *How do we make technology more capable without making it less human?*

Maybe that's why I'm fascinated by the little things we usually overlook —
the uncertainty, the context, the weird human decision, the unexpected interaction.

There is almost always a little bit of humanity hiding somewhere in the system.

**I like finding it.**
